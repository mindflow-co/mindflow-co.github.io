# Mindflow v0.9.2

**Released:** 2026-08-20

## Fixes

- **Arabic recordings no longer fill up with text that was never spoken.** Speech models are trained on subtitled video, and they memorise the credits that scroll at the end of it — lines like "Transcription and proofreading:" followed by a list of names. When there is nothing to transcribe but the model is asked to transcribe anyway — a call that is still ringing, hold music, a silent far end — it can reach for one of those memorised lines and write it into your transcript as if someone had said it. This was reported from a call nobody answered: the transcript showed a roster of three names that appears nowhere in the audio, twice.

  Mindflow now recognises that shape and removes it. Two things are worth knowing about how it decides, because both are deliberate.

  It only removes a line that appears **more than once, word for word, on the same side of the conversation**. A memorised credits roll is the same text produced again and again, so it repeats exactly; two things a person actually says almost never come out of transcription identical to the character. That means the first occurrence stays in your transcript. Removing it as well would mean holding lines back to see whether they repeat, and a transcript that arrives late is worse than one with a line of visible junk in it.

  And it will not touch anything you only said once. That matters more than it sounds: the phrasing of these credits lines — "transcription", "translation", "upload", "production" followed by a short list — is also how people dictate a list of tasks. If you say "transcribe the meeting, review the sprint, deliver the demo", that is a normal sentence that happens to look exactly like a credits roll, and it stays. Anything Mindflow removes is recorded in the transcript's data file, so nothing disappears without a trace.

## Under the hood

- Removing a hallucinated line no longer counts as the speech engine failing. Mindflow watches for the engine going quiet — returning nothing for audio it should be transcribing — and stops a recording that looks broken rather than let it run for an hour producing nothing. Discarded hallucinations were being counted as that same silence, which meant this fix, on its own, would have made a ringing call stop itself after 75 seconds — right before anyone picked up. The two are now counted separately: an engine that produces nothing still trips the safeguard on the same timings as before, while an engine producing text we choose to discard never does.
