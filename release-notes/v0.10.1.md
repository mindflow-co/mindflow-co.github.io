# Mindflow v0.10.1

**Released:** 2026-08-21

## Critical: the other person in your meetings was being recorded wrongly

If you recorded any meeting between **20 and 21 August**, the other participants will sound
distorted — too fast and high, or too slow and deep — and their words will be largely nonsense in
the transcript. Your own microphone was always recorded correctly; it is only the far side that was
affected. This release fixes it.

**Meetings recorded before 20 August are unaffected**, and meetings recorded from this version
onwards are correct.

Unfortunately the audio already saved for affected meetings cannot be repaired — the distortion
happened as it was captured, so the detail simply was not recorded. If any of those meetings matter,
they will need to be re-recorded.

## What went wrong

Mindflow changed how it captures other people's audio in v0.8.0, as part of moving to the narrower
"System Audio Recording" permission. That new method reads from a list of audio streams, and
Mindflow was reading the wrong one.

On a Mac using its built-in speakers there is only one stream, so it was always right — which is why
this was not caught in testing. But **headphones with a microphone in them, such as AirPods, add a
second stream**. On those, Mindflow was recording the headset's own microphone and labelling it as
the other participant, at roughly double speed.

A second, subtler problem sat behind it: Bluetooth headsets report one audio rate and then run at a
different one, which stretched recordings by about 9% even once the right stream was being read.
Mindflow now measures the real rate as it records rather than trusting what the device claims, so
this holds on any audio hardware regardless of its settings.

## Under the hood

- The capture stream is now identified by matching the tap's channel layout rather than assuming it
  is first in the list.
- Sample rate is verified against the wall clock during the first few seconds of every recording and
  corrected if the device misreports it.
- Mindflow now notices when a Bluetooth headset switches audio mode mid-meeting — which happens the
  moment a call starts — and re-configures instead of carrying on at the old rate.
