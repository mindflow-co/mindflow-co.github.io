# Mindflow v0.12.0

**Released:** 2026-08-24

## What's new

- **Tell Mindflow who was on the call.** A new **Speakers** button on any saved transcript lets you say how many people were on the other end and give each voice a real name. Speaker identification hears everyone on the far side through one mixed audio stream, so it genuinely cannot tell a quiet second participant from a single voice it has split in two — it guesses, and it prefers to merge, because one person under two names reads as broken. If it guessed wrong, you can now just say so, and the transcript re-renders with the right labels.
- **Names stick.** Both the speaker count and the names live in the transcript itself, so they survive re-renders, renames and folder moves.
- **Your assistant can fix speakers too.** Two new MCP tools, `get_meeting_speakers` and `set_meeting_speakers`, let Claude Code read who a meeting thinks was on the call and correct it — useful for fixing a batch of old transcripts in one go.

## Fixes

- **Playback is reliable again.** The play controls on a transcript could stop working — most often after switching from one meeting to another — while still showing the correct duration and scrubber, so nothing looked wrong. The audio player was being torn down by a view that no longer owned it. Play, skip and the per-line "play from here" buttons all keep working now, including across meeting switches.
- **"Detect automatically" now actually resets.** Choosing it after setting a speaker count silently did nothing; it clears the override properly.
- **Setting several names at once no longer loses one.** Each change re-renders the transcript, and rapid changes could overwrite each other. A count and every name are now applied as a single edit.

## Under the hood

- Speaker labelling has a single source of truth shared by the export and re-render paths, so a fix in one can no longer be silently undone by the other.
- No words ever change when speaker labels do — a re-render replays the current labelling rules over the original segment log. Verified on an 84-minute transcript: identical text, down to the word, before and after.
